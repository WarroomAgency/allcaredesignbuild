    Money Shouldn't Stop You From Building an ADU

    Many homeowners assume they need to have the full cost of an ADU saved before they can move forward. Financing can provide other ways to fund design, permitting and construction.

    San Diego homeowners have multiple financing paths. Depending on your equity, income, existing mortgage and project size, options may include home equity products, renovation loans, construction financing, grants and other lending solutions.

    Know The Difference

    Common Ways to Finance an ADU

    Each financing method works differently. Understanding the basic structure can help you have a more productive conversation with a lender.

    01

    HELOC

    A home equity line of credit allows you to borrow against available equity, generally giving you flexibility over how and when funds are used.

    Best suited for: Flexible project funding
    02

    Home Equity Loan

    A home equity loan provides a lump sum that is repaid through scheduled payments. It may be useful when you know the amount you need to borrow.

    Best suited for: Fixed-payment financing
    03

    Renovation Loan

    Renovation financing may consider the property's value after improvements, which can be useful for homeowners with limited existing equity.

    Best suited for: Qualified renovation projects
    04

    Construction Loan

    Construction financing is structured around the building process and may consider the property's projected value when the project is completed.

    Best suited for: Larger ADU projects
    05

    ADU Grants

    Certain California and local programs may provide financial assistance to qualifying homeowners. Availability and eligibility can change.

    Best suited for: Eligible homeowners
    06

    Cash Funding

    Using savings avoids loan interest, although homeowners should consider maintaining an appropriate emergency reserve before committing available cash.

    Best suited for: Homeowners with available savings
    Find Your Starting Point

    Which ADU Financing Option May Fit Your Situation?

    There isn't one financing solution that works for every homeowner. Start with your current financial position and project goals.

    01

    I have substantial home equity

    You may want to compare a HELOC or home equity loan with other available financing.

    02

    I have limited equity

    Ask lenders about renovation or construction financing that considers future property value.

    03

    I have a favorable existing mortgage

    Compare options carefully before replacing an existing mortgage with a cash-out refinance.

    04

    I have significant savings

    Self-funding may eliminate loan interest, but consider your overall cash reserves.

    05

    I want to explore assistance programs

    Check current state and local ADU programs to determine whether you meet their requirements.

    06

    I'm not sure where to start

    Start with your project scope and estimated budget, then discuss your situation with a qualified lender.
